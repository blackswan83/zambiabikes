Saddle up

Upcoming tours & events

Guided kid-sized rides all over Zambia — shady miombo loops, baobab ridgelines, the mist of Mosi-oa-Tunya and sunset gravel by Lake Kariba. Every single ride is helmet-checked, guide-led and backed by the Grown-Up Crew.

🛡️ How joining a ride works

  • 1. You ask. Tap "Request to join" on any ride — it sends a request, never a booking.
  • 2. Real grown-ups check. The Grown-Up Crew reads every request by hand. Nothing is automatic.
  • 3. Your parent or guardian confirms. They get an email from us, and only then is your spot locked in.

The calendar

Rides on the calendar

Small groups, big skies. Each card shows the distance, the ages it suits, and how many spots are left — the level chips go Easy Rider, Trail Star, then Downhill Hero.

More than rides

Club events

Some of our best days barely touch a trail: the Big Baobab Jamboree, the decorated-bike parade, and Trail Care Day — when we fix ruts, pick up every last wrapper and plant trees for shade in twenty years. Leave the trail better than you found it.

The Zambia Bikes standard

What every ride includes

Whether it's a gentle forest loop or the South Luangwa Big-Sky Pedal, the same six things happen on every single tour. No exceptions, no shortcuts.

🧭

Certified guides & the Grown-Up Crew

Trained adult guides lead from the front, and there's at least one grown-up for every four riders. Kids set the pace; adults hold the plan.

🪖

Helmets & a full gear check

No helmet, no ride — it's the first club rule. Before we roll, the crew checks every helmet, every brake and every tyre. Loaner helmets are always in the van.

🥭

Snack & water stops

Planned water breaks on every route, plus the famous mango break at the halfway point. In mango season, honestly, it's the best part.

🦅

Wildlife-wise briefings

Before we set off, a guide explains who we might meet — from fish eagles to hippos — and exactly how we keep a kind, sensible distance. In park areas, rangers ride with us.

🚲

A sweep rider at the back

A grown-up always rides last, so nobody is ever left behind. Slowest rider on the day? You've basically got your own personal guide.

🌳

The trail-care pledge

Every wrapper packed out, every rider on the trail and off the plants. We leave each trail better than we found it — that's the "ride kind" part of the motto.

Questions, answered

Good to know

The things riders and parents ask us most, before their first tour.

What bike do I need?

Any working bike with two brakes that fits you — a mountain bike is great, but plenty of riders start on whatever's in the shed. The crew checks every bike before we roll, and for skills days like the Copperbelt Pump Track Party we bring loaner bikes. If you don't have a bike at all, tell us in your request: the club's bikes-to-borrow fund exists exactly for that.

Can parents and guardians come along?

Yes — please do! Parents and guardians are welcome on every ride, either on a bike with us or at the start, snack stop and finish. Some of our best sweep riders started as "I'll just watch" parents. Sunset finishes, like the Baobab Ridge Rally, are made for family picnics.

How fit does my rider need to be?

If you can ride around your neighbourhood for half an hour, you can do an Easy Rider tour. Trail Star routes add hills and longer distances, and Downhill Hero rides are for confident riders aged 10 and up. Every card shows the distance and ages — and the sweep rider means nobody ever has to keep up.

What about wildlife safety?

Zambia's wild is why we ride — and we treat it with serious respect. Routes are scouted by adults before every tour, each ride starts with a wildlife-wise briefing, and in national-park areas we only ride with trained rangers. Hippos are admired from a very sensible distance.

What does a tour cost?

A small fee covers the guides, snacks, water and first-aid kit — your parent or guardian gets the exact amount in the confirmation email, so there are never surprises. And this matters to us: no kid is ever turned away for money. Just tell the Grown-Up Crew in your request and they'll quietly sort it out.

What if it rains?

Light rain means mud, and mud means the Mud Monster patch — we ride. Storms, lightning or flooded trails mean we don't: the Grown-Up Crew makes the call the evening before, and your parent or guardian gets an email with the new date. Safety picks the weather, not us.

First pick goes to members

Not a member yet?

Membership is free, and members get first pick of tour spots before rides fill up — plus patches, Trail Care Days and Zambia Rush bragging rights at every club showdown. Every sign-up is a request: the Grown-Up Crew reviews it and confirms with your parent or guardian before anything is unlocked.
