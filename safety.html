For parents & guardians

Serious about safety, silly about everything else

Zambia Bikes is fronted by two ten-year-olds — and safeguarded entirely by adults. This page was written with the Grown-Up Crew: the parents, certified guides and first-aiders who run vetting, safety and everything that matters. Here is exactly how it works.

The short version

Our four promises to you

Everything else on this page is detail. These four things never change.

🛡️

Vetted membership

Nobody joins automatically. Every sign-up is a request, read by a real adult on the Grown-Up Crew and confirmed with you — the parent or guardian — before a rider becomes a member. No confirmation from you, no membership. Ever.

⛑️

Guided & geared rides

Every tour runs with certified guides, a gear-and-helmet check at the start line, a first-aid trained adult in the group, and a sweep rider at the back so no child is ever last and alone. Routes are ridden by adults before kids ride them.

💬

A no-chat internet policy

There is no chat, no direct messaging and no friend-request system anywhere on this site or inside the Zambia Rush game. Kids here cannot talk to strangers through us, because we simply did not build a way to do it.

🔒

Your data stays small

We collect the minimum a bike club needs: a rider's first name and age, plus your contact details. No surnames from kids, no addresses, no schools, no phone numbers from children — and nothing is ever sold or shared.

Step by step

How vetting works

From the moment a request lands to the moment a rider is welcomed — and how you stay in control afterwards.

  1. A request arrives

    The join form asks for a rider's first name and age, plus your name and email as parent or guardian. A rider can optionally add their province, riding level and a line about why they love bikes — never a surname, school or address. The form itself requires a parent or guardian to fill it in or approve it, and you press send.

  2. A real adult reads it

    Requests go to the Vetting Desk of the Grown-Up Crew, not to a computer. A named adult reads every single one, usually within three days. There is no auto-approval anywhere in the process.

  3. We confirm with you first

    Before anything is unlocked — membership, tour bookings, the member patch — we email you directly to confirm the request really came with your blessing. Only your reply opens the door.

  4. You can pause or delete any time

    One email to hello@zambiabikes.org pauses a membership or deletes it completely, along with the data behind it. No forms, no questions, no waiting period.

Out in the wild

On the trail

What safety actually looks like on ride day, from the first helmet strap to the last mango break.

🪖

The helmet law

Our oldest rule and our only unbendable one: no helmet, no ride. Helmets are checked for fit at the start of every tour, and we keep spares in kid sizes for anyone whose lid doesn't pass.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑

One adult per four riders

Our guide ratio is 1 adult to 4 riders, always. Small groups mean every child is seen, heard and helped — and nobody has to shout to get a guide's attention.

🗺️

Routes ridden first

The Grown-Up Crew rides every route before every tour — checking trail conditions, water crossings and rest points that week, not last season. If a section isn't right for kids that day, we reroute.

⛑️

First aid on every ride

Every ride carries a full first-aid kit and at least one adult trained to use it — including Armand's mum, the club doctor, on the bigger tours. Scrapes get cleaned, wobbles get rested, parents get told.

🦒

Wildlife-wise rules

Kids learn to keep respectful distance and let animals move first. In national-park areas our guides ride with park rangers, and wildlife briefings happen before wheels roll, every time.

💧

Sun, water & snacks

Zambian sun is no joke. Sunscreen at the start, water stops on a schedule rather than on request, shade breaks on hot days, and a proper snack stop on every ride. Mango season makes this everyone's favourite rule.

The game

Online safety in Zambia Rush

Arthur designed the game; the Grown-Up Crew set its online rules. The design principle was simple: build nothing a stranger could use to reach a child.

🛡️ What the game can and cannot do

Zambia Rush is a free browser game. Kids race the clock, collect coins and try to beat Armand and Arthur's times. Here is the complete list of its online features — and its deliberate missing pieces:

  • No chat, no direct messages, no friend requests. These features don't exist in the game. There is no way for any player to send words to any other player.
  • Leaderboards show first names only. The device leaderboard lives on your own computer. When the site runs on the club's own server there is also a club leaderboard, shared only among club members: it holds a first name, a time and riding data, joining it requires typing a rider name first, and the Grown-Up Crew can remove any entry. There is no public global list of children's names.
  • Ghost Codes contain riding data only. A Ghost Code is a snippet of text kids can paste to race a friend's replay. It holds bike positions and a first name — nothing else. No messages can hide inside one, and the club server re-checks every code with the same rules before accepting it.
  • No accounts needed. Nobody registers, logs in or hands over an email address to play.
  • No ads, no purchases, no tracking scripts. There is nothing to buy, nothing to click away from, and no analytics or ad scripts anywhere. (The one outside service our pages use is Google Fonts, which serves the site's two typefaces.)

No legal maze

Privacy, plainly

Our whole privacy story fits in three short cards. If a privacy policy needs a lawyer to decode it, it is collecting too much.

📋

What we collect

A rider's first name and age, and a parent or guardian's name and email — plus, only if the rider chooses, their province, riding level and a few words about why they love bikes. That's the complete list. We never ask children for a surname, address, school or phone number.

🚵

Why we collect it

To organise rides: knowing who is coming, matching routes to ages, and reaching you — for confirmations, ride details and anything that comes up on the trail. That's it.

🚫

What we never do

We never sell or share data, and we never publish a child's details anywhere. Want it all gone? Email hello@zambiabikes.org and we delete everything, promptly and completely.

Straight answers

Questions parents actually ask

The five we hear most, answered plainly. Anything else — email us and a human replies.

Who exactly are the Grown-Up Crew?

Parents of club members — including Armand's mum, a doctor, and Arthur's dad, our mechanic — plus certified mountain-bike guides and trained first-aiders. Adults on the Crew run route planning, gear checks, first aid and the Vetting Desk. Armand and Arthur bring the ideas and the energy; the Crew holds every safety decision.

Can I ride along with my child?

Yes — please do! Parents and guardians are always welcome on tours, whether you're a seasoned rider or just fancy pedalling at the back with the sweep rider. Many of our best Grown-Up Crew members started as a parent who came along once and never stopped.

What does membership and riding cost?

Membership is free. Tours carry a small fee that covers guides, snacks and first-aid supplies — and no child is ever turned away over money. If the fee is a stretch, write to us quietly and we will sort it out; the club also runs a bikes-to-borrow fund for riders without their own wheels.

How do you handle photos of the kids?

No photo of your child is ever posted anywhere without your written OK first — and faces are always optional. Prefer helmet-and-handlebars shots only, or no photos at all? Just say so on the ride-day form and the Crew makes it happen. We also keep no public social-media accounts.

How do I remove our data?

Send one email to hello@zambiabikes.org from the address you registered with, and we delete your family's records completely — membership, request history, everything. We confirm by reply once it's done, normally within three days.

We're real people

Questions? Talk to a human.

No bots, no ticket numbers. Write to the Grown-Up Crew about anything on this page — or anything that isn't — and a real adult will write back.
