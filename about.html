Our story

Two friends, two bikes, one big idea

Zambia Bikes started with stabilisers, a pump track in Lusaka, and a promise made under a baobab: every kid in Zambia should get to feel what a good trail feels like. This is how we got here — and who keeps us safe along the way.

Meet the founders

The two bosses of Zambia Bikes

Both ten. Both bike-mad, game-mad and nature-mad. One plans the trails, one builds the fun — and neither goes anywhere without a helmet.

Armand, 10

Chief Trail Officer

Armand is the planner. He scouts every route with the guides, memorises every hill, and can tell you which trail rides best after the first rains. If a ride runs smoothly, it's usually because Armand drew it on paper three times first.

  • Favourite trail snack: roasted groundnuts and a just-ripe mango
  • Spirit animal: the African fish eagle — loud, proud, always watching the river
  • Proudest moment: cleaning the rock garden in Lusaka Forest — the whole thing, no foot down
  • Favourite sound: Mosi-oa-Tunya thundering when you're still a kilometre away
"A good trail is like a good friend. Look after it and it looks after you."

Arthur, 10

Chief Fun & Games Officer

Arthur is the inventor. He dreamed up Zambia Rush, draws every club patch by hand, and believes no ride is complete without at least one moment that makes everybody laugh. He also keeps The Snack Map — location classified, obviously.

  • Best trick: a tabletop — the backflip is happening in Zambia Rush first, then real life
  • Spirit animal: the chameleon — patient, colourful, sees everything
  • Club job: designs every patch riders earn, from First Ride to Mud Monster
  • Keeper of: the secret snack map of the three best mango trees in three provinces
"If it's not fun, we're doing it wrong. If it's not safe, we're not doing it at all."

How it started

From stabilisers to a whole club

Five years, four big moments, two best friends. Here's the short version.

  1. 2021

    Two five-year-olds meet at a pump track

    A dusty pump track in Lusaka. Armand and Arthur, both five, both wobbling. Their stabilisers came off in the very same week — and after that, nobody could get them off their bikes or away from each other.

  2. 2024

    The first big trail ride

    Their dads took them on a proper trail through the miombo woodland outside the city. Halfway up the climb, a fish eagle called overhead. Armand still says that was the exact second he knew bikes were going to be his whole life.

  3. 2025

    The idea under the baobab

    Resting in the shade of a giant baobab, mangoes in hand, one of them said it out loud: "Every kid in Zambia should get to feel this." By the time they got home they had a name, a plan, and a very long list of questions for their parents.

  4. 2026

    Zambia Bikes launches

    The parents said yes — with conditions. Certified guides, first-aid training, and grown-ups checking every single member. The Grown-Up Crew was born, the first tours went on the calendar, and Arthur shipped Zambia Rush so kids everywhere could ride Zambia, even before their first tour.

What we believe

Six things we'd put on a patch

Every club needs rules to ride by. These are ours — argued over, agreed on, and stitched into everything we do.

🪖

Safety first, always

Helmets on every head, guides on every ride, grown-ups checking every member. Being brave is great. Being safe is how you get to be brave again tomorrow.

🌳

Nature is the clubhouse

Miombo woodland, riverbanks, baobab shade — that's where we meet. So we keep it spotless and leave every trail better than we found it.

🚲

Every kid rides

No bike? No problem. Our bikes-to-borrow fund keeps a fleet of club bikes and helmets ready, so nobody misses a tour because of gear.

🎮

Games bring us together

Zambia Rush lets club friends race each other's ghosts without a single chat room. Rainy day? The downhill still runs.

💛

Kind is cool

We cheer the slowest rider the loudest. Kind words on the trail, kind words on the leaderboard — that's the whole policy.

🦅

Zambia is epic

The smoke that thunders, copper sunsets, fish eagles over Lake Kariba. We ride here because there is honestly nowhere better.

The Grown-Up Crew

The adults who make it all possible

Armand and Arthur run the ideas. Adults run the safety — full stop. The Grown-Up Crew is our team of certified ride guides, trained first-aiders and parents, including Armand's mum (the club doctor) and Arthur's dad (the club mechanic). Nothing happens on a trail, and nobody joins the club, without them.

🧭

Ride Guides

Certified mountain-bike guides who scout every route before tour day, lead from the front and ride sweep at the back. In national-park areas, they ride with rangers.

⛑️

Safety & First Aid

A trained first-aider — often the club doctor herself — rides with every group, kit packed. Gear checks before rolling, water and shade stops on the clock.

🛡️

The Vetting Desk

Real adults read every request to join, then confirm with a parent or guardian before any membership is unlocked. Nothing on this site is automatic — on purpose.

Trail-care pledge

Leave it better than you found it

Every rider says this pledge before their first tour. It's short, because the best promises are.

🌍 The Zambia Bikes pledge

  • Pack out every wrapper. If it rode in with us, it rides out with us — every crumb of every mango break.
  • Stay on the trail. The bush belongs to the animals and the plants; we're just visiting at speed.
  • Plant a tree each season. One rider, one tree, every season — the club has planted 128 so far and we're counting.

Ride wild, ride kind. That's the whole thing.
